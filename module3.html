Second-Order Response

This module shows how a second-order system (a mass-spring-damper, or an accelerometer) responds to a step input. Change the mass, damper and spring and watch how the response, the poles and the state-space model change together.

G(s) = K ωn² / (s² + 2ζωn s + ωn²)
ωn = √(k / m)     ζ = b / (2√(k m))     K = 1 / k
Important: The damping ratio ζ decides the shape of the response: ζ < 1 oscillates (underdamped), ζ = 1 is the fastest response without overshoot (critically damped), and ζ > 1 is slow with no overshoot (overdamped).

2. Poles in the s-plane

The poles are the roots of s² + 2ζωn s + ωn² = 0. Poles on the left side mean the system is stable. The farther the poles are from the imaginary axis, the faster the response dies out. The farther they are above and below the real axis, the faster it oscillates.

5. Understanding the Results

Underdamped step response (ζ < 1):

y(t) = K [ 1 − e−ζωn t / √(1−ζ²) · sin(ωd t + φ) ]
ωd = ωn √(1−ζ²)    φ = cos⁻¹(ζ)

Critically damped (ζ = 1):

y(t) = K [ 1 − (1 + ωn t) e−ωn t ]

Overdamped (ζ > 1):

y(t) = K [ 1 + (s₂ es₁t − s₁ es₂t) / (s₁ − s₂) ]
s₁,₂ = −ζωn ± ωn √(ζ²−1)

Overshoot and peak time (underdamped):

Overshoot = e−πζ / √(1−ζ²) × 100%    tp = π / ωd
Remember: The overshoot depends only on ζ. The speed of the response depends on ωn. Larger ζ gives less overshoot but a slower response.
