Bonus lab

Two experiments in controller design.

Two ways to shape a response

In experiment A, change the desired damping and watch both the poles and the response. Increasing the natural frequency usually speeds up this ideal model. In experiment B, try the critical-gain button to observe sustained oscillation, then apply the Z–N rules and compare how the response settles.

A · State feedback & pole placement

Reuse Module 3’s mass–spring–damper model, with x₁ = position and x₂ = velocity. Choose the desired damping and natural frequency. Control uses u = −k₁x₁ − k₂x₂ + Nr, with a reference prefilter for unit tracking.

The state-feedback experiment uses unlimited force and full state measurement. Selected pole locations describe this ideal model. Large gains can require impractical force on hardware.

B · Ziegler–Nichols ultimate gain

This experiment adds actuator and sensor lag to a motor model. The three cascaded lags are 1 s, 0.4 s and 0.2 s, giving G(s) = 1 / [(s+1)(0.4s+1)(0.2s+1)]. This additional model supports sustained oscillation at a finite proportional gain.

Critical gain follows the Routh stability boundary. The period is also measured from late positive peaks. PID rules use Kp = 0.6Ku, Ki = 1.2Ku/Pu, Kd = 0.075KuPu. A 0.01 s derivative filter is used, as in P1.
